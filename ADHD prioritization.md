### 1\. Leverage the ICNU Framework

Neurotypical prioritization often relies on "importance" and long-term "rewards." The ADHD brain, however, is driven by an interest-based nervous system. It naturally prioritizes based on Interest, Challenge, Novelty, and Urgency.

* Interest: Does this task spark curiosity?  
* Challenge: Is there a puzzle to solve or a game to be made out of it?  
* Novelty: Is there a new way to approach an old, boring task?  
* Urgency: Is the deadline absolute and imminent?

When evaluating a to-do list, look for the tasks that naturally contain these elements, or actively find ways to inject them into the tasks that do not.

### 2\. Externalize Working Memory

The ADHD brain frequently struggles to hold multiple steps and competing options in active working memory. This causes all tasks to feel equally massive and urgent simultaneously.

* The Brain Dump: Write every single task down on a physical piece of paper or a blank digital document. You cannot effectively prioritize while your brain is actively burning energy just trying to remember the list itself.  
* Visual Sorting: Once the list is out of your head, you can process it objectively.

### 3\. Filter by Negative Consequence

Because "importance" is a vague concept, it can easily lead to task paralysis when everything feels important.

* Instead of asking, "What is most important?", ask: "What blows up or causes a crisis if I don't do it today?"  
* If the answer is "Nothing," push it down the list. Let the reality of immediate, unavoidable consequences dictate your top slots.

### 4\. Build a Dopamine Snowball

Standard productivity advice often says to tackle the hardest, most dreadful task first ("Eat the Frog"). For someone with ADHD, staring down an under-stimulating, difficult task often leads to avoiding work entirely.

* Start with the easiest, most enjoyable, or most stimulating task on your list.  
* Completing it provides a hit of dopamine, which acts as the neurological fuel needed to initiate the harder, more tedious tasks. Use that momentum to carry you forward.

### 5\. Shrink the Task Scope

ADHD brains often reject vague abstractions and large-scale projects because the path to completion isn't immediately obvious.

* "Clean the house" or "Do the paperwork" are projects, not actionable tasks.  
* Break them down into micro-steps until the mental resistance disappears. "Take the three coffee cups from the desk to the sink" or "Open the spreadsheet and fill out the top row."

### 6\. Manufacture Urgency

If a task does not have a strict deadline, it essentially exists in the "Not Now" category of the ADHD timeline.

* Body Doubling: Work alongside someone else (virtually or in-person). Their presence creates immediate, gentle social pressure to stay on task.  
* External Accountability: Commit to showing your progress to a friend or colleague by a specific time today to force a deadline.
